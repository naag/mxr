CHAPTER 5 · SUBTRACTIVE SYNTHESIS

The filter: carving the sound

A sawtooth wave is bright and buzzy because it is full of harmonics. A low-pass filter lets the low ones through and blocks the high ones. Most synth bass sounds start bright and get carved down like this.

HEAR
A held E on a sawtooth. The envelope is off so the filter stays put.
SEE
Harmonics outside the teal curve fade out. The wave’s sharp edge rounds off.
DO
Drag the CUTOFF knob down slowly, then back up. Listen as you go.

THINK OF IT LIKE THIS

Put a blanket over a speaker. The bass still gets through, but the fizz and hiss disappear. Turning CUTOFF down is like adding thicker and thicker blankets.

Tell me more: why “4-pole”?

The pedal’s filter has four stages, called poles. Each one turns the sound down a bit more above the cutoff. Together they make it 24 dB quieter one octave above the cutoff, about 16 times quieter. That steep slope gives classic Moog and ARP synths their smooth, deep sound.

Takeaway: start with a bright wave and carve it down. That is subtractive synthesis, and CUTOFF is the carving knife.
